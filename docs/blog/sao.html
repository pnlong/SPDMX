Stable Audio Open fine-tunes

Question

Do matched-step Stable Audio Open (SAO) fine-tunes on SPDMX mixtures improve over Slakh under FAD and CLAP, and does the full corpus beat the BDGP-matched subset?

Setup

  • Model: Stable Audio Open 1.0, fine-tuned from the unwrapped pretrained checkpoint.
  • Budget: 10k optimizer steps per arm (checkpoint step=10000 used for generation).
  • Arms:
    • slakh — Slakh train mixes (~89 song-hours, 1,289 clips)
    • spdmx_matched — subset:bdgp (~38 song-hours, 632 songs)
    • spdmx_full — all SPDMX songs with a shipped mix (~5,469 song-hours)
  • SPDMX mixes are mono; SAO expects stereo — stable-audio-tools duplicates L/R at load time.
  • Eval: 100 held-out generations per arm (50 prompts × 2 seeds). FAD uses Slakh test reference mixes; CLAP is LAION-CLAP on the generations.

Metrics

  • FAD (↓): Frechet Audio Distance vs Slakh-test mixes (reference-sensitive).
  • CLAP (↑): LAION-CLAP score on held-out generations.

Slakh leads on both metrics under this Slakh-test reference setup. SPDMX BDGP-matched is close; full-corpus fine-tuning is worse on both. The main story is caption and content skew, not “more data is worse” in general: Slakh and BDGP-matched train almost entirely on multi-instrument captions (bass/drums/guitar/piano-style), while ~80% of SPDMX-full training captions are just instrumental music, piano (piano dominates the release; ~65% of songs are single-stem). Under a fixed 10k-step budget the full arm mostly reinforces that piano-heavy prior, so it sits farther from Slakh-test acoustics and from multi-instrument eval prompts.

Caveats: steps are matched, not unique-song exposure (batch sizes differed across arms in the recorded runs). Absolute CLAP gaps are small. Treat Slakh-referenced FAD as a domain-similarity signal, not a pure quality ranking.
