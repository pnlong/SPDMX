Experiment · Separation

Matched-budget Hybrid Demucs

Question

Under a fixed step budget, can Hybrid Demucs perform well on SPDMX in-domain, and how strongly does SPDMX training transfer across synthetic and recorded domains relative to Slakh2100?

Setup

  • Model: four-source Hybrid Demucs, trained from random initialization.
  • Targets: bass, drums, guitar, and piano (BDGP), following the Slakh2100 four-stem protocol. Keep only songs that contain all four targets.
  • SPDMX pool: 632 BDGP-eligible songs, shuffled at the song level into 600 training and 32 validation songs.
  • Arms: Slakh2100 alone | SPDMX alone | their concatenated union, sampled uniformly by song.
  • Budget: 32,000 updates, batch size 8, 10 s crops (mono duplicated to stereo).

Metrics

SI-SDR (dB, ↑): how closely an estimated stem matches its reference while ignoring overall loudness. Report bass–drums (BD) and guitar–piano (GP) means separately. Evaluate on Slakh2100 and SPDMX mixtures, and on recorded corpora: MUSDB18 (BD only), MedleyDB, and MoisesDB.

Single-dataset training is predictably strong in-domain and weaker across domains. The Slakh- and SPDMX-trained models are broadly comparable: SPDMX is better on MUSDB18 and comparable on MoisesDB, while Slakh is better on MedleyDB. Training on the union keeps similar in-domain performance and improves generalization on MoisesDB — the best of both worlds. Overall, SPDMX can be used as a standard source-separation dataset alongside Slakh2100.

Caveats: single matched-step run (no training-run uncertainty); not a scaling study on the full corpus; pitched-stem transfer to recordings remains weak. Treat this as a fixed-budget domain-gap check, not a separation SOTA claim.
