\begin{abstract}
Recorded multitrack stems remain scarce for modern separation and generative audio models, while large synthesized alternatives often depend on closed commercial instruments.
We present \textbf{SPDMX} (Synthesized PDMX), to our knowledge the largest public-domain multitrack stem audio corpus, derived from the PDMX symbolic collection.
Construction has three stages: score cleaning (General MIDI program correction; empty tracks removed), a listening study that selects an open FluidSynth / MIDI-DDSP render recipe, and full-corpus assembly with loudness- and velocity-aware mixing.
The release has approximately $254$K songs, $490$K mono stems, and $5{,}469$ hours at $44.1$\,kHz, sharing song identity with PDMX so symbolic metadata and scores pair with the audio.
Matched-budget Hybrid Demucs and Stable Audio Open fine-tunes provide training checks against Slakh2100 under SI-SDR, FAD, and CLAP.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Music source separation, accompaniment generation, automatic mixing, and stem-conditioned synthesis need paired mixtures and stems.
Vision~\cite{deng2009imagenet,schuhmann2022laion}, large-scale text~\cite{raffel2020exploring,gao2020pile}, and broader audio and speech~\cite{gemmeke2017audio,panayotov2015librispeech} have massive public corpora; music does not.
Commercial recordings are abundant, yet copyright bars most of them as open training data, especially paired stems.
Open recorded multitracks such as MUSDB18~\cite{rafii2019musdb18}, MedleyDB~\cite{bittner2014medleydb}, and MoisesDB~\cite{pereira2023moisesdb} are realistic but small (Table~\ref{tab:datasets}), even though more data and augmentation help separation~\cite{uhlich2017improving,pretet2019singing,stoter2018sigsep}.
Slakh2100~\cite{manilow2019cutting} scales synthesis with professional sample libraries, but those instruments are not redistributable, and $2{,}100$ songs / $145$ hours remain far below public-domain symbolic scale.

PDMX~\cite{long2024pdmx,xu2024generating} provides MusicXML and MIDI at public-domain scale (over $250$K scores, about $6{,}250$ hours).
Naive General MIDI (GM) soundfont renders of that material sound dry and synthetic; short soundfont notes poorly match acoustic sustain for winds, brass, and strings.
No public stem corpus yet renders that symbolic scale with open, redistributable instruments.

We introduce \textbf{SPDMX} (synthesized PDMX), a listening-tuned stem release from PDMX and, to our knowledge, the largest public-domain multitrack stem audio dataset: $253{,}982$ songs, $490{,}159$ mono stems, and $5{,}469$ hours of redistributable audio, song-aligned with PDMX and over $100{\times}$ more songs than Slakh2100.
Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0.15em}
\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
\setlength{\topsep}{0.35em}
\item We release that corpus with PDMX-aligned metadata and scores.
\item We define an open synthesis protocol with GM register correction, FluidSynth~\cite{fluidsynth} soundfont variety, selective MIDI-DDSP~\cite{engel2020ddsp,wu2022midi} for sustained instruments, and loudness- and velocity-aware mixing.
\item We run an ablation listening study on perceived realism to choose the production render recipe.
\item We report matched-budget Hybrid Demucs~\cite{defossez2021hybrid} and Stable Audio Open (SAO)~\cite{evans2024stable} training checks against Slakh2100.
\end{enumerate}
Upon acceptance, we will make the dataset and code public.\footnote{\url{\demourl}.}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:prior}

Open recorded multitrack datasets~\cite{rafii2019musdb18,bittner2014medleydb,pereira2023moisesdb} are small, and prior large-scale stem synthesis has used closed commercial instruments.
Slakh~\cite{manilow2019cutting} is the closest precedent, which rendered Lakh MIDI~\cite{raffel2015large,raffel2016learning} with closed VSTs to study how data quantity and quality affect separation.
SPDMX keeps that stem-and-mixture setup, but renders public-domain scores with \emph{open} synthesizers at PDMX scale: over $100{\times}$ more songs and about $38{\times}$ more hours than Slakh, and fully redistributable (Table~\ref{tab:datasets}).

MIDI-DDSP~\cite{wu2022midi}, a DDSP-based~\cite{engel2020ddsp} model trained on URMP~\cite{li2018creating}, showed that neural MIDI-to-audio can yield high-quality synthetic corpora, continued by the Chamber Ensemble Generator~\cite{wu2022chamber}.
We use that approach where listening favors it over FluidSynth soundfonts.

\section{SPDMX Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}

SPDMX has three stages: clean PDMX scores, choose an open render recipe by listening, then assemble the full release under that recipe.

\subsection{Initial data cleaning}
\label{ssec:cleaning}

PDMX MIDI programs often disagree with track names (e.g., a named violin encoded as piano).
We correct GM programs from track-name aliases before rendering and drop tracks with no note events.
Fig.~\ref{fig:gm_programs} shows the corrected inventory by stem count and non-silent hours: for each rendered stem we sum only $0.25$\,s windows whose RMS amplitude is at least $0.01$ ($\approx -40$\,dBFS), excluding near-silent gaps between notes and trailing padding.
Mass concentrates in piano, choir/voice, drums, and common orchestral families.
Piano dominates stem count ($57\%$) more than non-silent hours ($41\%$): many scores include a piano track, but those parts are often sparse or short, so they contribute less audible time per stem than sustained choir or orchestral lines.

Naive single-bank GM rendering remains dry and synthetic, and short soundfont notes poorly match acoustic sustain for winds, brass, and strings.
That motivates listening among open FluidSynth banks and selective MIDI-DDSP.

\subsection{Listening study}
\label{ssec:listening}

We use listening tests to choose a render recipe for each instrument category.
Every track becomes a mono stem at $44.1$\,kHz.
The \emph{basic} soundfont strategy uses one GM bank (\texttt{SGM-V2.01}).
The \emph{varied} strategy samples a shortlist of redistributable soundfonts per category, including \texttt{FluidR3\_GM}, \texttt{GeneralUser GS}, \texttt{Arachno}, and \texttt{Airfont}, plus a light reverb FX profile, for Slakh-like timbral variety without proprietary libraries.
For eligible monophonic strings, brass, and winds, MIDI-DDSP~\cite{wu2022midi} can replace the soundfont; we fall back to FluidSynth for polyphonic parts, instruments outside the MIDI-DDSP (URMP) inventory, and all other GM programs.
Voice-class tracks use choir or voice soundfonts (lyric singing synthesis is out of scope).

We compare four families: basic, varied, MIDI-DDSP over basic fallbacks, and MIDI-DDSP over varied fallbacks.
The open reference is the basic soundfont, and when present, it also appears as a hidden reference among shuffled unlabeled conditions.
Each trial is a 10-second stem excerpt scored for perceived realism ($0$--$100$).
The set has two stems in each of ten categories ($20$ pages) and $N{=}8$ listeners (Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation_listening}).

We freeze one path per category, choosing the highest mean by default.
When basic only narrowly beats varied, we keep varied for diversity.
SPDMX uses basic for drums, organ, and guitar, and varied for piano, voice, mallet, and residual classes; strings and brass use MIDI-DDSP over basic, and wind uses MIDI-DDSP over varied ($\star$ in Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation_listening}).

\subsection{Final assembly}
\label{ssec:assembly}

The frozen recipe is applied to every cleaned song under redistributable synthesizers.
Independent per-stem loudness matching would erase MIDI velocity balance, so each stem is loudness-normalized toward $-23$\,LUFS~\cite{series2011algorithms} with peak limiting, then scaled by the ratio of that track's maximum note-on velocity to the song maximum.
A single anti-clip peak gain on the stem sum, applied uniformly, keeps stems linearly summable (mixture equals stem sum)~\cite{manilow2019cutting}.

SPDMX has $253{,}982$ songs and $490{,}159$ mono FLAC stems ($5{,}469$ hours), plus paired MIDI.
Of these, $88{,}403$ ($34.8\%$) have more than one stem (mean $1.93$; max $66$; Fig.~\ref{fig:stems_per_song}).
A BDGP-eligible slice (bass, drums, guitar, and piano present) supports matched Slakh comparisons.
Songs share identity with PDMX (MusicXML, metadata, and audio without separate alignment) and use only open synthesizers.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We verify SPDMX with two matched-step training studies: Hybrid Demucs~\cite{defossez2021hybrid} source separation and Stable Audio Open~\cite{evans2024stable} fine-tuning.

\subsection{Source separation}
\label{ssec:separation}

Hybrid Demucs~\cite{defossez2021hybrid} recovers bass, drums, guitar, and piano from coherent mixtures.
For a head-to-head with Slakh2100, we map SPDMX parts to those classes and keep songs with all four (about $36$\,h of SPDMX training audio vs.\ $83$\,h of Slakh under our manifests).
We compare three matched-step arms: Slakh, SPDMX (BDGP), and Slakh~$\cup$~SPDMX (BDGP).
Table~\ref{tab:separation} reports mean SI-SDR~\cite{leroux2019sdr}.
In-domain, SPDMX (BDGP) is learnable, and the union largely keeps Slakh-test performance while transferring to SPDMX (BDGP).
Solo arms fail cross-domain, consistent with prior evidence that synth-trained separators transfer poorly to mismatched acoustics~\cite{manilow2019cutting,bugler2020study}.
MUSDB18 bass and drums remain hard for all synth-trained arms, as in Slakh-only training on MUSDB~\cite{manilow2019cutting}.

\subsection{Generative fine-tuning}
\label{ssec:sao}

SPDMX is mainly a large public-domain audio corpus with paired MIDI, so mixtures support generative fine-tuning beyond the four-stem filter.
SAO~1.0~\cite{evans2024stable} is fine-tuned from the public checkpoint on loudness-normalized mixtures with simple instrumental captions under three matched-step arms: Slakh, SPDMX (BDGP), and full SPDMX.
Held-out generations for a fixed prompt set ($n{=}100$) are scored with VGGish Fr\'{e}chet audio distance~\cite{kilgour2018fr,cramer2019look} against Slakh held-out mixes and LAION-CLAP~\cite{wu2023large} (Table~\ref{tab:sao}).
The matched SPDMX arm is close to Slakh; full-corpus fine-tuning under the same step budget is weaker on both metrics.
FAD favors Slakh-like acoustics here because the reference set is Slakh held-out mixes, and FAD is known to be sensitive to reference choice~\cite{gui2024adapting}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Paired mixtures and stems remain scarce for open research: recorded multitracks are small, and prior large synthetic corpora rely on closed commercial instruments.
SPDMX addresses that gap by turning public-domain PDMX scores into redistributable multitrack audio with open FluidSynth banks, selective MIDI-DDSP where listening favors it, and loudness- and velocity-aware mixing.
The release has $253{,}982$ songs, $490{,}159$ mono stems, and $5{,}469$ hours at $44.1$\,kHz, song-aligned with PDMX, and over $100{\times}$ more songs than Slakh2100.
Matched-step Hybrid Demucs and Stable Audio Open fine-tunes show that SPDMX is learnable in-domain and usable beside Slakh under SI-SDR, FAD, and CLAP, offering a fully open path to stem supervision at public-domain scale.
We release the corpus and render recipe to support separation, generative, and stem-conditioned work at public-domain scale.
Future work includes larger listening studies, stronger generative evaluations, and better MIDI-to-audio methods for more expressive, realistic synthesis.
Beyond our explorations here, we hope SPDMX enables large-scale work on stem-conditioned and accompaniment generation, automatic mixing, and joint symbolic and audio modeling without copyrighted recordings.

\section{Compliance with Ethical Standards}
\label{sec:ethics}

SPDMX is derived from public-domain PDMX and released for open research redistribution.
Rendering uses redistributable soundfonts and open MIDI-DDSP weights, not closed commercial VST sample libraries.
