\begin{abstract}
Recorded multitrack stems remain scarce relative to the data needs of modern separation and generative audio models, while large synthesized alternatives often depend on closed commercial instruments.
We present \textbf{SPDMX}, a public-domain multitrack stem corpus derived from the PDMX symbolic collection.
Scores are cleaned (General MIDI program correction; empty tracks removed) and rendered with open soundfont synthesis and selective MIDI-DDSP for sustained orchestral families.
Render choices are selected with a listening study on perceived realism.
The release comprises approximately $254$K songs and $490$K mono stems at $44.1$\,kHz, sharing song identity with PDMX so symbolic metadata and scores can be used with the audio.
Matched-budget Hybrid Demucs and Stable Audio Open fine-tunes compare Slakh2100 against four-stem and full SPDMX under objective SI-SDR, FAD, and CLAP.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Music source separation, accompaniment generation, and related audio models consume paired mixtures and stems.
Recorded corpora such as MUSDB18~\cite{rafii2019musdb18}, MedleyDB~\cite{bittner2014medleydb}, and MoisesDB~\cite{pereira2023moisesdb} supply realistic multitracks, yet remain small in song count and duration (Table~\ref{tab:datasets}).
Slakh2100~\cite{manilow2019cutting} enlarges training volume by synthesizing MIDI with professional sample libraries, but those virtual instruments are not redistributable, and the release is far smaller than contemporary public-domain symbolic collections.

PDMX~\cite{long2024pdmx} contributes MusicXML and MIDI at public-domain scale.
Naive General MIDI (GM) soundfont rendering of that material yields dry, recognizably synthetic stems; for sustained winds, brass, and strings, short soundfont notes are a particularly weak match to acoustic sustain.
The missing piece is an open path from those scores to stem-level audio that researchers can train on without closed VST stacks.

We introduce \textbf{SPDMX}, a listening-tuned stem release built from PDMX.
Contributions:
(i)~a large public-domain multitrack audio corpus aligned in song identity with PDMX;
(ii)~an open synthesis protocol combining GM register correction, FluidSynth~\cite{fluidsynth} soundfont variety, and selective MIDI-DDSP~\cite{engel2020ddsp,wu2022midi} where soundfonts underserve sustained instruments, with loudness and velocity-aware mixing;
(iii)~an ablation listening study on perceived realism that selects the production render recipe;
(iv)~matched-budget Hybrid Demucs~\cite{defossez2021hybrid} and Stable Audio Open (SAO)~\cite{evans2024stable} proof-of-concept evaluations against Slakh2100.
Upon acceptance, the dataset and all related code will be made publicly available.\footnote{\url{https://pnlong.github.io/SPDMX/}.}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:prior}

Slakh~\cite{manilow2019cutting} is the closest precedent: MIDI from Lakh~\cite{raffel2015large,raffel2016learning} is rendered with professional sample libraries to study how training data quantity and quality affect separation.
That design yields usable synthetic stems at roughly an order of magnitude beyond MUSDB18, but reproduction depends on closed VSTs.
SPDMX targets a similar stem-and-mixture training interface from public-domain scores and redistributable open synthesizers.

Recorded multitrack sets~\cite{rafii2019musdb18,bittner2014medleydb,pereira2023moisesdb} remain the realism reference for evaluation and augmentation studies.
On the synthesis side, MIDI-DDSP~\cite{wu2022midi} (built on DDSP~\cite{engel2020ddsp}; trained on URMP~\cite{li2018creating}) showed that neural MIDI-to-audio can produce high-quality synthetic corpora from symbolic scores to fill gaps in recorded music data, a line continued by the Chamber Ensemble Generator for MIR~\cite{wu2022chamber}.
We adopt that precedent selectively, employing MIDI-DDSP where category-level listening favors it over soundfonts, while using FluidSynth for the remainder of the GM inventory.

\section{SPDMX}
\label{sec:dataset}

PDMX MIDI often carries program numbers that disagree with track names (e.g., a named violin track encoded as piano).
We correct GM programs from track-name aliases before rendering.
Tracks with no note events are dropped so released stems correspond to parts that actually play.
Fig.~\ref{fig:gm_programs} compares program mass before and after correction on non-empty tracks.
After correction, inventory concentrates in acoustic piano, choir/voice, drums, and common orchestral winds, brass, and strings, which motivates concentrating listening effort and neural synthesis on those families.

  Empty tracks are removed before release.}
  \label{fig:gm_programs}

Each remaining track is rendered to a mono stem at $44.1$\,kHz.
Two soundfont strategies appear in the ablation:
\emph{basic} uses a single default GM bank (\texttt{SGM-V2.01});
\emph{varied} samples a listening-selected shortlist of redistributable soundfonts (and a light FX profile) per instrument category, including \texttt{FluidR3\_GM}, \texttt{GeneralUser GS}, \texttt{Arachno}, and \texttt{Airfont} among others, aiming for Slakh-like timbral variety without proprietary sample libraries.
The complete soundfont inventory is documented with the code and project page.
For eligible monophonic strings, brass, and winds, MIDI-DDSP~\cite{wu2022midi} replaces the soundfont when listening selects a neural path; we fall back to FluidSynth for polyphonic parts, instruments outside the MIDI-DDSP (URMP) inventory, and all other GM programs.
Lyric-conditioned singing-voice synthesis is out of scope; voice-class tracks use choir/voice soundfonts.

Independent per-stem loudness matching would erase relative track levels that MIDI velocities encode.
Each stem is first loudness-normalized toward $-23$\,LUFS~\cite{series2011algorithms} with peak limiting, then scaled by the ratio of that track's maximum note-on velocity to the song-level maximum.
A single anti-clip peak gain, computed on the sum of scaled stems and applied uniformly, keeps stems linearly summable, so the mixture equals the stem sum~\cite{manilow2019cutting}.

On a fixed probe set spanning piano, drums, strings, wind, voice, mallet, organ, guitar, brass, and residual polyphonic classes, listeners rated blinded render variants for perceived realism while soundfont shortlists and FX profiles were locked per category.
A subsequent formal study compared four non-neural-to-neural render families without audio-to-audio post-enhancement (Sec.~\ref{sec:listening}).
Listening scores inform the frozen hybrid recipe but do not dictate it alone: when basic only narrowly outranks varied, we prefer varied to retain timbral diversity.
The release uses varied soundfonts for piano, voice, and mallet; basic soundfonts for drums, organ, guitar, and residual polyphony; and MIDI-DDSP (with soundfont fallback as above) for strings, brass, and wind, so every song remains complete.

SPDMX contains $253{,}982$ songs and $490{,}159$ mono FLAC stems ($5{,}469$ hours of score-aligned material), plus paired MIDI for the released tracks.
Of these, $88{,}403$ songs ($34.8\%$) have more than one stem (mean $1.93$ stems/song; max $66$), so a large multitrack subset sits alongside abundant single-part material.
Songs share identity with PDMX: the same works expose MusicXML, symbolic metadata, and audio stems without a separate alignment procedure.
Unlike Slakh, rendering uses only redistributable open synthesizers rather than closed commercial sample libraries.

\section{Listening study}
\label{sec:listening}

Four render families were compared: basic soundfont (A1), varied soundfonts (B1), MIDI-DDSP over basic fallbacks (CA1), and MIDI-DDSP over varied fallbacks (CB1).
The open reference was A1; when present, the same signal also appeared as a hidden reference among shuffled unlabeled conditions.
Each trial showed a 10-second stem excerpt; listeners scored every condition for perceived realism ($0$--$100$).
The trial set used two stems in each of ten instrument categories ($20$ pages).
$N{=}8$ listeners scored the full ablation battery; Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation_listening} reports mean realism for the four families retained for the release (when a neural condition reused a soundfont stem unchanged, that stem inherited the donor condition's score).

A1 attains the highest mean realism; CA1 is the strongest neural-augmented option.
Per-category patterns guide the hybrid in Sec.~\ref{sec:dataset}, with close calls resolved toward variety rather than strictly maximizing the mean score.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

Two objective proof-of-concept studies use a matched optimizer step budget.
No preference listening is collected for these arms.

\subsection{Source separation}
\label{ssec:separation}

Hybrid Demucs~\cite{defossez2021hybrid} is trained to recover bass, drums, guitar, and piano---Slakh's non-vocal benchmark targets---from coherent mixtures.
For a fair head-to-head with Slakh, we map SPDMX parts onto those four classes and keep only songs that contain all four.
That slice is much smaller than the full multitrack release, so we compare three matched-step arms: Slakh alone, the four-stem SPDMX subset alone, and their union (Slakh+SPDMX), asking whether SPDMX complements Slakh rather than replacing it.
The remaining multi-stem material (orchestral, voice, and other instrument families) is still useful as an open complement to Slakh for separation settings that are not limited to those four targets.
Evaluation reports SI-SDR~\cite{leroux2019sdr} on the Slakh2100 test set (Fig.~\ref{fig:downstream}, left).

\subsection{Generative fine-tuning}
\label{ssec:sao}

Unlike Slakh2100, which is organized around separation targets, SPDMX is primarily a large public-domain \emph{audio} corpus with paired MIDI: mixtures support generative fine-tuning and related tasks even for single-stem songs or songs outside the four-stem filter above.
SAO~1.0~\cite{evans2024stable} is fine-tuned from the public checkpoint on loudness-normalized song mixtures with simple instrumental captions under three arms: Slakh train, the same four-stem SPDMX songs used for separation, and full SPDMX.
Held-out generations for a fixed prompt set are scored with OpenL3-based Fr\'{e}chet audio distance~\cite{kilgour2018fr,cramer2019look} and LAION-CLAP~\cite{wu2023large} prompt adherence (Fig.~\ref{fig:downstream}, right).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

SPDMX converts public-domain PDMX scores into open multitrack stem audio at the scale of the parent symbolic corpus, using listening-selected FluidSynth and MIDI-DDSP rendering with loudness- and velocity-aware mixing.
Ablation listening supports a hybrid production recipe; a three-arm Demucs comparison (Slakh, SPDMX, Slakh+SPDMX) and full-corpus SAO fine-tunes situate the release relative to Slakh2100 under SI-SDR, FAD, and CLAP, while the wider multi-stem and single-stem inventory remains available as an open complement for separation and other audio tasks.
Broader listener pools and stronger generative evaluations remain open.
